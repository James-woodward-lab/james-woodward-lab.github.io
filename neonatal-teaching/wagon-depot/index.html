    How to read the town
    • CO₂ wagons bring both outlaws and sheriffs. CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. One wagon, one of each.
    • pH reports the number of outlaws roaming free. More free outlaws means lower pH. pH 7.40 is 40 nmol/L of H⁺; pH 7.10 is 80 nmol/L.
    • The ratio, not the count, sets pH. A town with 40 sheriffs and twice the wagons has the same pH as a normal town. That is why the Sheriff-to-Wagon Ratio Dial sits beside the Town Order Meter.
    • The Marshal’s Posse removes CO₂ wagons. Breathing faster clears wagons in minutes. Breathing less lets them pile up.
    • The Sheriff Recruitment Office adds sheriffs over time. The kidneys take days to recruit (or stand down) sheriffs, so a chronic wagon surge has a far better pH than an acute one.
    • Bunkhouse deputies temporarily contain outlaws during an acute wagon surge. Haemoglobin and proteins buffer H⁺ within minutes, which is why bicarbonate rises by only about 1 mmol/L per 10 mmHg acutely.
    • Base excess fixes the wagons at 5.3 kPa and asks how many sheriffs are missing or spare. Missing sheriffs mean a posse has been through (metabolic acidosis). Spare sheriffs mean a sheriff train or a busy Recruitment Office (metabolic alkalosis or renal compensation).

    Rules of thumb: acute +1 mmol/L HCO₃⁻ per 10 mmHg rise (−2 per 10 mmHg fall); chronic +3.5 per 10 mmHg rise (−5 per 10 mmHg fall). Winter’s formula for expected PaCO₂ in metabolic acidosis: 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 (±2) mmHg.

    Units and limits

    kPa are used throughout; mmHg appear in small print. 1 kPa = 7.5 mmHg. The equation constant is 0.03 mmol/L per mmHg, which is 0.225 per kPa (shown as 0.23). The sprite counts are scaled to keep the town readable: one wagon per 5 mmHg, one sheriff per 2 mmol/L, one free outlaw per 5 nmol/L. The numbers on the dials are the real values.

    Teaching aid only. The model uses textbook compensation rules and a simplified Van Slyke base excess. It is not for clinical decision making.
